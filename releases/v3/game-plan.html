01

The bet

Cats are a proven, low-CPI theme. Idle is the genre with the best casual stickiness on record. The gap is that today's hits are pleasant but not compulsive, and their share loops are weak. That's the wedge.

Idle players open the app more times per day, sit in ~8-minute sessions, and respond to a well-understood retention toolkit: offline earnings, login streaks, collection gaps, and events. Cat themes cut user-acquisition cost because the creative tests well cold. Put those together and the unit economics are favorable before a single novel idea is added.

02

What the category already taught us

Four teardowns. Each one contributes a mechanic to copy and a mistake to design around.

Neko Atsume

Kitty Collector · Hit-Point Co.

Take: zero-pressure loop. Cats never leave, never die, nothing punishes absence. Return is driven purely by curiosity — "which cats showed up?" — a variable reward.

Take: the collection book (the Catbook) is the whole progression spine. Visible gaps pull you back.

Avoid: almost no monetization surface and no live-ops. It plateaus. We keep the calm but add depth and events.

Cats & Soup

Idle village · HIDEA

Take: tight tap → sell → upgrade → unlock loop with constant soft feedback (animation, sound, particles) so every action lands.

Take: decoration + themed areas as an evergreen money and retention sink; a Netflix ad-free tier extends reach.

Avoid: ad-heavy monetization that can feel relentless. We cap frequency and make every ad opt-in and rewarding.

Simon's Cat — Crunch Time / Match

Puzzle + meta · Tactile / Strto

Take: a strong pre-existing IP voice and expressive character animation carry the first session.

Avoid: bolting a generic match-3 onto the license. The cat is set dressing, not the mechanic. Our cats must be the gameplay.

The Battle Cats

Tower-offense gacha · PONOS

Take: absurd, screenshot-worthy character design creates free organic sharing and a decade-long tail.

Avoid: mechanical complexity and a steep wall. Wrong audience for cozy — but the lesson about memeable characters transfers directly.

03

The game

You run a small seaside cove that stray cats wander into. You make it comfortable; they stay, do cat things, and produce goods. You collect, decorate, and — the new part — capture the moments.

Core loop

01AttractPlace food, toys, cozy spots. Each attracts different cat personalities.
→
02TendOne satisfying active beat: brush, feed, or the "here kitty" gather tap. Big tactile payoff.
→
03CollectCats produce yarn, shells, fish. Tap to gather; offline earnings while away.
→
04InvestSpend on new breeds, rooms, décor themes, and comfort upgrades that raise output.
→
05CapturePhoto Mode snapshots an emergent scene. Frame it, caption it, share it.

Loop time  15–40 s to feel a full turn · 3–6 min median session · designed for 4–8 opens per day.

Differentiator 1 — Cats with a personality model

Every cat carries 2–3 trait tags (e.g. Shy · Glutton · Climber · Chatty) drawn from a small pool. Traits drive lightweight autonomous behaviour: where they sit, what they knock over, who they befriend or avoid, what décor they react to.

This turns a static diorama into a system that generates unscripted cute moments — the raw material for both attachment and sharing. Cheap to build (state machine + weighted tables), high perceived depth.

Differentiator 2 — The Photo Mode viral loop

A one-tap Photo Mode freezes the scene, offers frames/stickers/caption, and exports a clean square image with a small watermark and an install deep link.

Category ASO now rewards "satisfying / cozy / shareable" language over genre keywords. A built-in reason to post is a compounding, near-zero-cost acquisition channel — the thing Neko Atsume got by accident, engineered on purpose.

Design guardrail — the calm rule

No fail state. No cat ever leaves or suffers from neglect. No timers that punish. Absence costs you potential gains, never progress. This is the single most important thing Neko Atsume proved and the easiest thing to break under monetization pressure.

04

Meta systems & the retention ladder

The core loop is the 10-second hook. These layers are what turn a week of play into a season.

Day 1 — the hook
  • First cat within 20 seconds, no tutorial wall
  • First décor unlock inside 3 minutes
  • Photo Mode surfaced after the 2nd cat
  • Offline-earnings popup on 2nd open ("your cats were busy")
Day 7 — the habit
  • Login streak with escalating treat rewards
  • Daily "wandering visitor" — a rare cat you can coax to stay
  • Catbook 30–40% full; clear next 3 targets shown
  • First weekend micro-event (themed cat + décor set)
Day 30 — the collection
  • Room 2 / biome 2 unlock as a fresh goal surface
  • Monthly event cadence with limited cosmetics
  • Prestige-style "cove rating" for long-tail optimizers
  • Light social: visit a friend's cove, leave a treat, get a postcard
  • Currencies: soft (shells, earned freely) · premium (pearls, bought or earned slowly) · event tokens (time-limited). Keep it to three, ever.
  • Collection: ~60 cats at launch across 4 rarity bands; breed variants (coat, eyes, patterns) multiply perceived content cheaply.
  • Décor: themed sets (Tidepool, Lighthouse, Greenhouse…) that are both self-expression and output multipliers — the primary IAP sink.
  • Live-ops: one major themed event per month, one micro-event per weekend, authorable from a config file so a solo dev can run it.
05

Monetization

Cozy/lifestyle titles in 2026 skew roughly 59% IAP / 41% ads. Lead with IAP and a subscription; treat ads as an opt-in convenience, never a toll.

IAP ~58%
Rewarded ads ~34%
Sub ~8%
IAP — currency packs, décor bundles, one-time starter pack Ads — rewarded video only, opt-in Subscription — "Cove Club"

Rewarded placements

opt-in, capped, always a clear trade

· 2× offline earnings on return
· Re-roll the daily wandering visitor
· Instant-finish a décor build
· Bonus photo frames / sticker packs

No forced interstitials in the first 3 sessions. After that, at most one on app-background, frequency-capped.

In-app purchase

the main line

· $2.99 starter pack (best-value, one-time)
· $0.99–$49.99 pearl packs
· $3.99–$7.99 themed décor bundles
· Seasonal event pass (cosmetic track)

Everything buyable is cosmetic or a time-shortcut. Nothing is gated behind a paywall permanently.

Cove Club — subscription

$4.99 / month

· Removes all interstitials
· Daily pearl stipend (nets out > cost for engaged players)
· One exclusive club cat per month
· 2× base earnings always on

Targets the 5–10% of players who want to support the game and skip friction.

Ethical monetization = retention

2026 data is consistent: games that feel fair keep players and monetize better over the life of the cohort. Practical rules — show odds on any randomized pull, never sell power in a game with no competition, cap ad frequency hard, and make the subscription cancellable in two taps.

Ad mediation

when the time comes

AppLovin MAX or Unity LevelPlay — both bidding-first in 2026, both clear the market on rewarded eCPM. Start with one, add AdMob as a bidder. Rewarded fill >95% once wired correctly.

Backend

keep it thin

Local-first save (device) with cloud backup via a BaaS (PlayFab, Supabase, or Firebase). Server-authoritative only for the social layer and event config. No always-online requirement.

Analytics

instrument from day one of soft launch

GameAnalytics or Unity Analytics for funnels + retention; a lightweight event schema (loop_complete, cat_collected, photo_shared, ad_watched, iap_view/purchase). Watch D1/D7, session count, ad ARPDAU.

07

Build roadmap

Staged so every phase has a cheap exit. Solo-dev / tiny-team estimates; AI-assisted asset and code workflows are assumed and typically cut 15–25% off schedules.

01

Playable prototype — the fun test

~4 weeks · 1 devStack: Web / PhaserSpend: ~$0–4K

Greybox one biome: attract → tend → collect → invest loop, 6–8 placeholder cats, the personality state machine, offline earnings. No art polish, no monetization.

Exit: 5–10 testers voluntarily reopen it across 3+ days. If the loop isn't quietly compelling in greybox, stop or re-cut here.

02

Vertical slice — the look and the feel

6–10 weeksStack: Unity 6Spend: ~$15–40K (mostly art/audio)

Rebuild the loop in Unity with final art direction for one biome, ~15 cats, Photo Mode v1, sound design, juice pass. Store page assets and one CPI creative test.

Exit: CPI test under target for the theme, and the vertical slice reads as "cozy hit" to outside eyes. Art direction locked.

03

Soft launch — the numbers

8–12 weeks liveMarkets: PH / CA / AU / NZSpend: ~$20–60K incl. UA

Full content for 2 biomes, ~40 cats, all monetization wired, live-ops config, analytics. Iterate D1/D7, tutorial funnel, and first-purchase conversion weekly.

Exit gate: D1 ≥ 40%, D7 ≥ 15%, early ARPDAU + payer conversion trending to a positive LTV:CPI. Miss twice → rescope or shelve.

04

Global launch

OngoingFeaturing pitch to Apple / GoogleSpend: scales with ROAS

60+ cats, 3–4 biomes, monthly event calendar, Custom Product Pages per keyword cluster (cozy / collection / cats / ASMR). Scale UA only on proven positive ROAS.

Exit: not a gate — a mode. Live-ops cadence sustains the cohort; new biomes quarterly.

05

08

Risks & kill criteria

Named up front so they're decisions, not surprises.

The loop isn't compulsive in greybox

Signal: testers don't reopen the prototype unprompted.

Response: this is the cheap kill point. Re-cut the active beat or the reward cadence once; if still flat, don't proceed.

Photo sharing doesn't spread

Signal: <3% of DAU export a photo; k-factor negligible in soft launch.

Response: the game must still stand on paid UA + retention alone. Treat virality as upside, not the plan.

CPI too high despite cat theme

Signal: creative tests don't beat category benchmark.

Response: iterate creative against the "satisfying moment" first frame; if economics still don't close, shelve before soft launch.

Art scope creep

Signal: per-cat art cost balloons; content velocity for live-ops unsustainable.

Response: lock a modular cat rig (shared skeleton, swappable coats/patterns) in the vertical slice. Non-negotiable.

09

Immediate next steps

The first two weeks.

  • Lock the concept one-pager — pin the biome (seaside cove vs. cottage garden vs. bookshop), the active beat, and the three currencies.
  • Build the web prototype — Phaser + TypeScript greybox of the full loop and the personality state machine. Target: playable in ~4 weeks.
  • Design the economy on paper — earnings curve, upgrade costs, offline-rate formula, first-week unlock pacing. A spreadsheet model before any balancing in-engine.
  • Run a creative pre-test — 3–4 mock store creatives of the "satisfying moment," cheap paid test for click-through, before committing to art direction.
  • Draft the cat bible — trait pool, ~15 launch cats with personalities, rarity bands, the modular rig spec.
Recommendation

Green-light Phase 1 only. A four-week web prototype is the entire risk of finding out whether this is a hit-shaped loop, and it costs almost nothing to build or to walk away from. Everything after it is a numbers decision.
